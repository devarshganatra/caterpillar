## Overview

Cab temperature and ambient heat affect an operator's reaction time and
judgment well before they feel seriously unwell. Heat stress is
progressive: early signs like reduced concentration and slower reactions
show up long before heat exhaustion symptoms, and by the time obvious
symptoms appear, decision-making is already impaired.

## Why Breaks Are Scheduled By Heat Index, Not Just Temperature

Ambient temperature alone understates the real load on an operator; the
combination of temperature and humidity (heat index) is what actually
predicts physiological strain. This is why break prompts trigger off a
heat index threshold rather than a raw temperature reading, and why the
same air temperature can be fine on a dry day and require more frequent
breaks on a humid one.

## Machine Heat And Operator Heat Are Linked

A cab with poor air conditioning compounds ambient heat directly, and an
engine running hot (approaching its own health thresholds) often means the
whole machine — including the cab — is running hotter than normal. A
health threshold warning on engine temperature is also a cue to check
whether cab conditions have degraded.

## Recommended Response

Take scheduled breaks during high heat-index periods even if you feel
fine — the point is to prevent the decline, not react to it. Increase
hydration during hot shifts, and treat rising engine temperature alongside
personal fatigue as a combined signal to pause rather than push through.
