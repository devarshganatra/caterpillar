## Overview

A pre-start walkaround exists to catch problems while they are still cheap
and safe to fix — a slow hydraulic leak found at 6am is a five-minute
delay; the same leak discovered mid-lift is a much bigger problem. The
checklist is short on purpose: it targets the failure modes most likely to
cause an incident that same shift.

## What The Checklist Actually Catches

Typical items include visible fluid leaks, tire or track condition,
seatbelt and mirror condition, backup alarm function, and visible damage
to guards or the ROPS/FOPS structure. Each of these maps directly to a
specific failure mode: a leak becomes a fire or slip hazard, a damaged
guard exposes a moving part, a non-functional backup alarm removes a
ground crew's only warning of machine movement.

## Why A Failed Item Blocks Shift Start

Any single failed item on the checklist is treated as blocking, not
advisory, because the checklist was deliberately kept to items where
"operate carefully around it" is not an acceptable substitute for fixing
the problem. A machine with a failed item should be flagged for
maintenance, not started on a "monitor it" basis.

## Recommended Response

Complete the full checklist before every shift start, even on a machine
you operated yesterday — machines develop new issues overnight, and
skipping the walkaround because "it was fine yesterday" defeats its
purpose. If an item fails, report it and do not start the shift on that
machine until it is resolved or a supervisor formally overrides it.
