## Overview

Trenching and bulk excavation productivity depends heavily on matching
bucket fill technique and swing pattern to the material being dug. The
same excavator can vary in cycle time by a large margin between loose,
easily-dug soil and dense, rocky, or heavily compacted material — this is
expected variation, not a performance problem by itself.

## Bucket Fill Factor

A bucket that is only partially filled each cycle requires more cycles to
move the same volume of material, directly increasing task duration even
if each individual cycle looks normal. Consistently low fill (visible as
more cycles than the target-cycle estimate expected) is one of the most
common, fixable causes of a task running longer than predicted.

## Swing Efficiency

Minimizing swing angle between the dig and dump positions — by
positioning the truck or spoil pile as close to the dig area as
practical — has a large cumulative effect on cycle time over a full task.
A gradual increase in average cycle time partway through a task can
indicate the truck or spoil position has drifted further from the optimal
swing angle.

## Ground Conditions And Digging

Wet or muddy ground affects excavation differently than it affects travel:
it can make material stickier and harder to release cleanly from the
bucket, which slows cycles even though the machine itself is not moving
any slower. This is a distinct effect from the travel-speed impact of mud
described in the adverse weather guidance.

## Recommended Response

If a task's ETA is slipping, check target-cycle progress against elapsed
time before assuming the whole task will run proportionally longer — a
slow start with a recoverable swing-position fix is different from a
sustained material-difficulty slowdown.
