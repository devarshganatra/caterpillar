## Overview

Struck-by incidents involving ground personnel are one of the leading
causes of serious injury around excavators and loaders. Proximity
detection systems divide the space around a machine into zones — commonly
a red (immediate danger, stop) zone and an orange (caution, slow and
verify) zone — and warn the operator when a person or obstacle is detected
inside them.

## Why Zone Radii Change With Conditions

A fixed detection radius is wrong in most real conditions. Rain and low
visibility reduce how quickly a person can react to a moving machine, and
mud or wet ground increases stopping distance. Widening the zones under
these conditions is not overcaution — it restores the same effective
reaction margin the base radius was designed to give in good conditions.

## Reading An Orange Vs Red Alert

An orange (warning) detection means a person or obstacle is close enough
that the operator should slow down, sound the horn if unsure, and confirm
the area is clear before continuing the current motion. A red (critical)
detection means someone is inside the minimum safe distance for the
machine's current motion — the correct response is to stop that motion
immediately, not to finish the current pass first.

## Repeated Detections In A Short Window

If the same zone triggers repeatedly within a short window, this usually
means a haul truck, spotter, or ground crew is working routinely close to
the machine for that task — not that the sensor is malfunctioning. Treat a
consolidated repeat alert as confirmation that a person has been in the
zone for an extended period, and coordinate directly with them rather than
dismissing further alerts.

## Recommended Response

Reduce swing or travel speed near marked work zones, use a spotter for
blind-side moves, and never assume a previous "all clear" still applies
after the machine has moved.
