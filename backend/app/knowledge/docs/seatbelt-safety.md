## Overview

Seatbelts are the single most effective protection against ejection during a
rollover or sudden stop on heavy equipment. Most seatbelt-related incidents
on excavators and loaders happen not because the belt was forgotten, but
because it was removed for a "quick" repositioning move and never
refastened before real work resumed.

## Why It Matters On Tracked And Wheeled Machines

Excavators and loaders have a high center of gravity relative to their
track or wheel base, and can tip during side-slope travel, uneven ground
crossing, or an overload swing. A properly fastened seatbelt keeps the
operator inside the ROPS/FOPS-protected cab envelope during a rollover;
without it, the operator's body can move outside that protected zone even
during a short, low-speed event.

## Ingress And Egress Grace

It is normal and expected for the belt to be unfastened for a few seconds
while the operator boards or leaves the cab with the engine idling. A
short grace period for this is appropriate. What is not appropriate is
travelling or working — swinging the boom, curling the bucket, moving the
tracks — with the belt off.

## Common Root Causes

- Belt removed to reach something in the cab, then not refastened before
  the next move
- Belt mechanism worn or the latch not seating audibly, encouraging
  operators to skip it
- A false sense of safety after many incident-free shifts

## Recommended Response

If a seatbelt violation is flagged while the machine is travelling or
working, stop non-essential motion, refasten the belt, and only then
continue. If the buckle will not latch reliably, take the machine out of
service and report it — do not compensate by "being extra careful" instead.
